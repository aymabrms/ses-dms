# Classification Conflicts

This document identifies classification differences in the questionnaires and executive summary. These should not be silently normalized during implementation.

| Classification | Source questionnaire(s) | Conceptual conflict | Why it matters | Possible normalization strategies | Risks of normalization | Decision now? | Domain-expert question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Employment status | Household members: Permanent, Contractual, Temporary, Unemployed, Too young/old to work. Business employees: Permanent, Temporary, Others. Executive summary: Employed, Self-employed, Unemployed, Not Applicable/Not in Labor Force with minors/students/retired. | Different taxonomies and levels of detail. | Reporting employment status requires mapping from collection categories to report categories. | Maintain source values plus a reviewed reporting category mapping. | Forced mapping may misclassify contractual/temporary as employed or not. | No. | What official employment-status categories should be used for reporting, and how do source choices map? |
| Civil status | Household members: Single, Married, Annulled, Common Law/Live-in, Widowed, Single parent. Business employees: Civil Status free/table field. Business owner has Civil Status. Executive summary: Single, Married, Separated, Widow/Widower, Common-law or live-in. | Annulled, separated, widow/widower, widowed, single parent differ. | Civil-status report requires consistent categories. | Store raw source choice and map to canonical civil status after review. | `Single parent` is not strictly civil status. | No. | Should single parent be a civil status, vulnerability, household role, or separate attribute? |
| Household relationship | Household member table: Household Head, Wife/Spouse, Son/Daughter, Grandson/Granddaughter, Father/Mother, Relative, Non-relative. Executive summary: Household head, Spouse, Son/daughter, In-law, Niece/Nephew, Grandchild, Parent, Brother/Sister, Other relative, House helper, Not related. | Executive summary is more granular than questionnaire table. | Household structure report may require categories not collected exactly. | Expand questionnaire categories in future or allow detailed relationship lookup. | Existing source data cannot reliably split `Relative` into niece, sibling, in-law, etc. | No. | Which relationship categories are authoritative: questionnaire or executive summary? |
| Respondent relationship | Household/Landowner relationship options use Household Head, Wife/Husband, Mother/Father, Daughter/Son, Others. Business asks relationship to business owner. | Relationship target differs. | A single lookup would lose target context. | Use relationship type with target role context. | Generic values could imply wrong relationship. | Yes: keep target context. | Are relationship options intentionally limited for proxy respondents? |
| Educational attainment | Household member field is open/table heading. Business employee field is open/table heading. Executive summary has detailed categories including preparatory, vocational, apprenticeship, no response, not applicable. | Report categories are more detailed than visible questionnaire choices. | Reporting may need categories not constrained in paper form. | Use controlled education lookup aligned to reporting in future questionnaire engine. | Legacy paper values may be inconsistent free text. | No. | What exact education choices were used by enumerators or encoders? |
| Skills categories | Household and employee skill list: carpentry, plumbing, welding, beauty care, cooking, sewing, driving, basic computer operation, mechanic, industrial painting, computer technician, hair dressing, food processing, handicrafts making, others. Business livelihood training list: construction related, food preparation/technology, hotel/restaurant related, automotive related, etc. | Trade skills and sector training categories differ. | Skills reports and livelihood planning can be distorted if lists are merged. | Separate skill taxonomy from training-sector taxonomy; map later if needed. | Merging may hide important preference differences. | Yes: keep current skill and preferred training distinct. | Should trade skill choices and vocational training sectors be reported together or separately? |
| Occupation | Household member primary occupation; Landowner occupation; executive summary uses major occupation groups. | Source may collect free text, report uses standardized groups. | Occupation reporting requires coding. | Store raw occupation and reviewed coded occupation category. | Automatic coding can be wrong. | No. | Who will code occupations to official categories, and which standard applies? |
| Income representation | Household member monthly income amount, household monthly income bracket, household head source categories, business average monthly income, employee salary by frequency, executive summary monthly income distribution. | Multiple measurement subjects and formats. | Aggregation/reporting requires denominator and subject clarity. | Use typed income records with subject, period, amount/bracket, and source context. | Treating all income as monthly household income would be invalid. | Yes: do not collapse. | Which income fields feed the executive summary income distribution? |
| Expense representation | Household expenditure by category and frequency, household monthly expenditure bracket, business average monthly expenditures, executive summary monthly expenses distribution. | Category/frequency detail differs from aggregate bracket; business and household expenses differ. | Expense reports need consistent source. | Store expense context and frequency; define reporting transformation later. | Summing mixed frequencies incorrectly can distort totals. | No. | Is monthly expense distribution based on aggregate bracket or computed from category expenses? |
| Salary frequency | Business employee table has Daily, Weekly, Monthly columns under Salary in PhP. | Frequency may be mutually exclusive or multiple filled. | Employee compensation cannot be normalized without frequency rule. | Capture amount plus selected frequency; flag multiple frequency entries for validation. | Assuming monthly may overstate/understate salaries. | No. | Should enumerators choose exactly one salary frequency per employee? |
| Rent concepts | Household rent expense, structure rent received, respondent-household rent paid, business occupancy rent, business land rent, employee-quarter rent, landowner land/structure rental received. | Same term covers paid expense, received income, and occupancy charge. | Financial reports and compensation eligibility may differ. | Model rent with payer/payee/asset/context/frequency. | Single rent field would be unusable. | Yes: keep rent contexts separate. | Which rent values are required for compensation or reporting? |
| Ownership type | Business ownership: single proprietorship, partnership, corporation, others. Land ownership: individual, community ownership, corporation. Land type: private, public, right-of-way, ancestral domain. Structure ownership: own Yes/No. | Ownership choices vary by asset type. | Ownership affects eligibility and workflow. | Separate ownership taxonomies by asset type. | Generic ownership lookup would mix legal meanings. | Yes: separate by domain. | Are land ownership type and land type both required for all land records? |
| Land use | Household/Landowner current actual use: residential, commercial, industrial, agricultural, others. Business asks type of structure occupied, not land use. | Land use and structure use are similar but not identical. | Structure/business reporting needs correct domain. | Keep land use separate from structure use and business nature. | Merging could imply land zoning from structure use. | Yes. | Are multiple current land uses allowed? |
| Structure use/type | Household structure use: residential, commercial, industrial, others; type: single detached, duplex, townhouse, others; associated structure type: fence, shed, garage, toilet, pig pen, others; Business structure type: commercial, commercial-residential, industrial, institutional, residential-institutional. | Use, building type, and associated structure type are mixed across questionnaires. | Structure inventory and impact assessment need consistent definitions. | Separate structure use, dwelling/building type, associated-structure type, material, condition. | One generic structure type would lose meaning. | No. | What authoritative structure classification should be used in DMS? |
| Financial institution membership | Household: Pagibig, SSS, Cooperative, GSIS, Others. Business employee: Pag-IBIG, GSIS, SSS, Cooperative, PhilHealth, Micro-finance, Others. Household government programs also include PhilHealth. | PhilHealth appears as program/benefit in one source and financial membership in another. | Reporting and assistance eligibility may differ. | Store source context and institution/program type. | Reclassifying PhilHealth may change counts. | No. | Should PhilHealth be treated as financial institution membership, government program access, or both? |
| Government programs | Household asks Scholarship, 4Ps/CCT, PhilHealth, Supplemental Feeding, Subsidized rice, Housing program, Others. | Not asked in Business/Landowner. | Program access reports may be household-only. | Keep as household program access unless expanded. | Applying to all roles would create false missing data. | Yes for current sources. | Are government program questions required for business owners or landowners in later versions? |
